Tracking breathing exercises with earphones

IEEE EMBC 2024 · PDF · arXiv · IEEE

Can normal wireless earphones tell if someone is doing their breathing exercises right?

Problem

Breathing exercises are part of treatment for asthma, COPD, high blood pressure, anxiety, and more. Doctors want to know if patients actually do them.

In a clinic, someone can watch. At home, nobody can.

What we built

An app that listens to breathing through the earphone microphones. Every half second, two small neural networks answer two questions:

  • Nose, mouth, or pause?
  • Breathing in or breathing out?

That is enough to check an exercise like "in through the nose, out through the mouth" as it happens.

Why

Earphones: lots of people already own them, and they sit close to the nose and mouth. Nothing new to buy.

Half-second clips: short enough that feedback feels instant.

Two networks: two separate questions, so each network only has to learn one thing.

Result

Nose vs. mouth vs. pause scored up to 98% (F1). Breathing in vs. out scored up to 89%. The paper was published at IEEE EMBC 2024.

It shows that cheap hardware people already have can help with therapy at home.
